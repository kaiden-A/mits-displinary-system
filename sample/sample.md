This flowchart describes a **school disciplinary complaint and action process** — starting from a complaint/report or a discipline spot-check, continuing through investigation and recording of the offence, and finally ending with disciplinary action and, where necessary, a meeting with the student's parent/guardian.

I'll break it down **from top to bottom**, including what each part means.

---

# 1. Overall purpose of the flowchart

The chart shows how a school should handle a **student disciplinary case**.

There are actually **three possible ways a case can enter the system**:

1. **Complaint from teacher / parent / guardian**
2. **Warning card issued by a school prefect**
3. **Offence discovered by the discipline teacher through a spot check**

These three routes eventually **merge into one main disciplinary process**.

A simplified version is:

```text
Complaint / Warning / Spot Check
              ↓
       Investigation
              ↓
     Record the offence
              ↓
      Student admission
              ↓
      Disciplinary action
              ↓
       Principal approval
              ↓
       School punishment
              ↓
   Notify parent/guardian
              ↓
      Parent called?
        ↙           ↘
      YES            NO
       ↓              ↓
 Parent meeting      END
       ↓
 Record meeting
       ↓
      END
```

---

# 2. Route A — Complaint from teacher / parent / guardian

On the **left side** of the diagram:

### Step 1 — Complaint form is completed

> **Guru / ibu bapa / penjaga mengisi Borang Aduan (B01) (Jika ada).**

A teacher, parent, or guardian who notices a disciplinary problem can make a formal complaint.

They fill in:

**B01 — Borang Aduan**

The "(Jika ada)" means this form is used **if there is a complaint/report**.

For example:

> A teacher notices that a student repeatedly violates school rules.

The teacher can submit a complaint.

---

### Step 2 — Complaint is handed to the Discipline Teacher

> **Serah Borang Aduan kepada Guru Disiplin**

The completed B01 is given to the school's **Guru Disiplin**.

The Discipline Teacher becomes responsible for handling the case.

---

### Step 3 — Investigation

> **Jalankan siasatan dan ambil tindakan jika aduan berasas (B02)**

The Discipline Teacher investigates the complaint.

This is important because **a complaint does not automatically mean the student is guilty**.

The school first needs to determine whether the complaint has a reasonable basis.

The investigation may involve things such as:

* talking to the student
* talking to witnesses
* checking relevant evidence
* checking school records
* confirming what actually happened

If the complaint is found to be **berasas** (well-founded), disciplinary action can proceed.

The chart labels this with **B02**.

---

# 3. Route B — Prefect issues a warning card

The **middle/top route** is different.

### Step 1 — Prefect issues Kad Peringatan

> **Pengawas mengisi Kad Peringatan (B03) untuk murid yang langgar peraturan sekolah.**

A **school prefect (Pengawas)** sees a student violating a school rule.

The prefect fills in:

**B03 — Kad Peringatan**

For example:

> A student violates a minor school rule.

The prefect records the incident using the warning card.

---

### Step 2 — Warning card is given to relevant people

> **Serah Kad Peringatan kepada:**
>
> * Murid
> * Ketua Guru Disiplin / Guru Disiplin

The warning card is given to:

1. **The student**
2. **Head of Discipline / Discipline Teacher**

So there is a record that the prefect observed the violation.

---

### Step 3 — Student is referred to discipline authority

> **Pelajar berjumpa GPK HEM / Ketua Guru Disiplin / Guru Disiplin**

The student then meets one of the appropriate disciplinary authorities:

* **GPK HEM** — Penolong Kanan Hal Ehwal Murid
* **Ketua Guru Disiplin** — Head of Discipline
* **Guru Disiplin** — Discipline Teacher

This allows the school to decide what should happen next.

---

# 4. Route C — Spot Check

On the **right side**:

> **Guru Disiplin membuat pemeriksaan berkala / mengejut (Spot Check)**

The Discipline Teacher can independently conduct:

* regular inspections, or
* surprise inspections

This is a **Spot Check**.

For example, the school might inspect:

* students' belongings
* uniforms
* prohibited items
* compliance with school rules
* other disciplinary requirements

Unlike Route A, this doesn't necessarily start because somebody submitted a complaint.

The teacher **discovers the problem directly**.

---

# 5. All three routes merge

This is an important part of the flowchart.

The three routes:

```text
Complaint
    ↓
Investigation
    ↘
      ─────────→ Main disciplinary process
    ↗
Warning Card
    ↓

Spot Check
    ↓
```

eventually join at the same point.

The next step is:

> **Guru Disiplin merekod kesalahan dalam Buku Rekod Disiplin (B04)**

The Discipline Teacher records the student's offence in:

**B04 — Buku Rekod Disiplin**

This becomes the school's disciplinary record for the case.

---

# 6. Student fills in the confession/admission form

The chart then says:

> **Murid mengisi Borang Pengakuan Murid (B05) bagi kesalahan 10 mata dan ke atas**

The student fills in:

**B05 — Borang Pengakuan Murid**

but specifically for offences worth **10 points or more**.

So there is a threshold:

```text
Disciplinary offence
       ↓
Is it 10 points or more?
       ↓
      YES
       ↓
Student completes B05
```

This means B05 isn't necessarily required for every minor offence.

---

# 7. Discipline Teacher takes action

Next:

> **Tindakan Guru Disiplin**

The flowchart lists several things the Discipline Teacher may do.

### A. Issue SPMS card

> **Isi Kad SPSM (LAM/DIS/002-1)**

The Discipline Teacher records the case using the relevant **SPSM card/form**.

This creates an official disciplinary record.

---

### B. Prepare notification/warning letter

> **Isi Surat Pemberitahuan / Surat Amaran kepada ibu bapa / penjaga (B06) (jika berkaitan)**

If the case requires it, the school prepares:

**B06 — Surat Pemberitahuan / Surat Amaran**

This is addressed to the:

* student's parent, or
* guardian.

"Jika berkaitan" means **if applicable**.

Not every disciplinary case necessarily requires a parent warning letter.

---

### C. Prepare an undertaking letter

> **Menyediakan Surat Akujanji (B08) jika berkaitan**

The school may also prepare:

**B08 — Surat Akujanji**

This is an undertaking/commitment document, where applicable.

Essentially, it documents the student's commitment to comply with school rules or the conditions imposed by the school.

---

# 8. Headmaster/Principal signs the letter

Next:

> **Guru Besar tandatangan Surat Pemberitahuan/Amaran kepada ibu bapa/penjaga (jika berkaitan)**

The **Guru Besar (Headmaster/Principal)** signs the notification or warning letter.

So the process isn't simply:

```text
Discipline Teacher → Parent
```

Instead:

```text
Discipline Teacher
       ↓
Prepare letter
       ↓
Guru Besar signs
       ↓
Letter can be sent
```

This gives the communication an official school authorization.

---

# 9. School Discipline Body carries out punishment/action

Next:

> **Badan Disiplin Sekolah melaksanakan hukuman/tindakan dengan merujuk Modul SPSM**

The **School Discipline Committee/Body** carries out the appropriate disciplinary action.

Importantly, the punishment/action is supposed to refer to the **SPSM module**.

So the school should not simply invent a punishment arbitrarily.

Conceptually:

```text
Offence
  ↓
Determine appropriate action
  ↓
Refer to SPSM guidelines/module
  ↓
Carry out disciplinary action
```

---

# 10. Letter is delivered to parent/guardian

Next:

> **Hantar Surat secara serahan tangan/pos**
>
> **(makluman melalui telefon jika perlu tindakan segera)**

The school sends the letter to the student's:

* parent, or
* guardian.

The delivery can be:

### Option 1 — Hand delivery

The letter is physically handed to the parent/guardian.

### Option 2 — Post

The letter is sent through postal delivery.

### Option 3 — Telephone notification

If the situation requires **immediate action**, the school can notify the parent/guardian by telephone.

Notice that the telephone notification doesn't necessarily replace the formal letter; it's described as a way to provide **immediate notification**.

---

# 11. Decision: Is the parent/guardian called?

Now we reach the **diamond**, which represents a decision.

> **Ibu bapa / Penjaga dipanggil?**

Meaning:

> **"Is the parent/guardian called to come to the school?"**

There are two possible outcomes:

### YA = YES

The parent/guardian **is called**.

The flow goes left.

### TIDAK = NO

The parent/guardian **is not called**.

The flow goes downward.

---

# 12. If YES — Parent/guardian meeting

If the answer is **YA**, the process becomes more involved.

The chart says:

> **Buat pertemuan dengan ibu bapa/penjaga**

The school arranges a meeting with the parent/guardian.

This allows the school to discuss the disciplinary case directly with them.

---

# 13. Parent/guardian signs the undertaking letter

During the meeting:

> **Tandatangan Surat Akujanji (B08) (jika berkaitan)**

If applicable, the parent/guardian signs the **B08 Surat Akujanji**.

So the meeting isn't just a discussion — there can also be formal documentation.

---

# 14. Meeting outcome is recorded

After the meeting:

> **Rekod butiran / hasil pertemuan dalam Kd SPSM**
>
> **(LAM/DIS/002-1)**

The school records:

* details of the meeting
* what was discussed
* the outcome/result
* relevant actions

in the **SPSM record**.

This is important because the school now has a documented history of what happened.

---

# 15. If NO — directly finish

If the decision is:

> **TIDAK**

meaning the parent/guardian does **not** need to be called,

the process goes directly toward:

> **TAMAT**

So the process ends after the disciplinary action and notification process.

---

# 18. If you're turning this into a software system

Since you're working on a **school RBAC/discipline system**, this flowchart is actually very useful because it can be translated into a **case workflow**.

I'd model the central object as something like:

```text
Disciplinary Case
```

with a lifecycle such as:

```text
REPORTED
   ↓
INVESTIGATING
   ↓
CONFIRMED
   ↓
RECORDED
   ↓
STUDENT_ACKNOWLEDGEMENT
   ↓
DISCIPLINARY_ACTION
   ↓
PRINCIPAL_APPROVAL
   ↓
ACTION_EXECUTED
   ↓
PARENT_NOTIFIED
   ↓
 ┌─────────────────┐
 │ Parent called?  │
 └─────────────────┘
       ↓       ↓
      YES      NO
       ↓        ↓
    MEETING    CLOSED
       ↓
    CLOSED
```

And you could keep the **source** separately:

```text
case.source

COMPLAINT
PREFECT_WARNING
SPOT_CHECK
```

while the documents are linked to the case:

```text
case
 ├── B01 complaint
 ├── B02 investigation
 ├── B03 warning card
 ├── B04 discipline record
 ├── B05 student acknowledgement
 ├── B06 parent notification
 └── B08 undertaking
```

That would make the system much cleaner than treating every form as a completely separate process. The **disciplinary case is the central record**, while B01/B03/B04/etc. are documents/actions attached to that case.
